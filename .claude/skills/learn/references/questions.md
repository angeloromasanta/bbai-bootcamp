# Writing a fair question

A question is a measuring instrument. A sloppy one measures how well I guess.

## Building the options

1. **Work out the right answer first.** State it as a bare claim: no qualifier, and
   never the word "because". If any option contains "because", "since" or "so that",
   rewrite the set: the reason belongs on the next screen.
2. **Derive each wrong option from a real mistake**: two neighbouring ideas mixed up, a
   square forgotten, a direction reversed, a rule applied outside its conditions. Give
   it the same form, length and level of detail as the right answer. The wrong option I
   pick should tell you what I believe.
3. **Put all reasoning in the explanation**, on the next screen. An option that carries
   its own justification gives itself away.
4. **Four options, and the right one goes where this list says.** The page letters
   them A to D in the order you write them and adds "I don't know" as E. Nothing
   shuffles, and left alone you will put the right answer first every time. So count
   your questions in this lesson and place the right answer by this cycle:
   question 1 → C, 2 → A, 3 → D, 4 → B, 5 → B, 6 → D, 7 → A, 8 → C, then repeat.
5. **Read it cold.** If someone who does not know the material could pick the answer
   because it is the longest, the most careful, or the only one with a technical term,
   rewrite the whole set.

Avoid "all of the above", "none of the above", "which is NOT", and trick wording.

## What to ask

- Test use, not recall. "What happens to the spread if n is four times larger?" beats
  "What is the formula for the standard deviation?"
- Each question depends on my last answer. Do not prepare a list in advance.
- A small computation I can do in my head is fine. Anything needing paper is too long.
- One question per screen, and nothing on that screen hints at the answer.

## After I answer

Open the next screen with the verdict:

- **Right:** say so, and give the reason in one line. If the reasoning I typed was
  wrong even though the letter was right, say that too: it counts as a miss.
- **Wrong:** "Not quite, it is 40 and 60." Say why that holds, then name the confusion
  behind the option I picked.
- **I don't know:** no judgement. Tell me the answer in one line, record it as a gap,
  and drop to an easier question on the same prerequisite. The first time, tell me this
  is the most useful answer I can give: it shows you exactly where my edge is.
- If I answer in words instead of a letter, grade the words.

## In the chat (only when the page is not used)

```
Q3. X ~ Binomial(10, 0.5). What is Var(X)?

- A) 5
- B) 2.5
- C) 25
- D) sqrt(2.5)
- E) I don't know

Reply with a letter. Add a line on your reasoning if you like.
```

Each option is its own list item, because the chat joins plain lines into one
paragraph. Always add **E) I don't know** yourself here.
